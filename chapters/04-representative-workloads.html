Chapter 04Chapter outlineLab planned

Choose a representative workload

Does your recommendation change when the data and usage resemble the customer’s?

This is a chapter outline. The worked example and executable lab are planned. You can use the design task below to start your benchmark brief.

What you will learn

  • Translate customer operations into a workload specification.
  • Vary query mix, selectivity and data distribution.
  • Explain which scenarios the evidence represents.

01 / The question

Start with a customer decision

The first comparison uses uniformly distributed orders. The customer has a few unusually large accounts and a mixture of recent-order lookups and historical reports.

02 / Worked example · planned

The comparison to build

Keep the orders scenario and compare a uniform dataset with one concentrated on a small group of customers. State how the synthetic data relates to the customer.

03 / Experiment · planned

How the lab will work

  1. Specify data volume, key distribution, query mix and arrival pattern.
  2. Change one workload dimension and repeat the two-engine comparison.
  3. Compare separate operation types before choosing any weighted summary.

The executable lab, reference outputs and progressive hints will be added when this chapter is implemented.

04 / Design task

Start your benchmark brief

Design a workload from a customer scenario. Record the evidence for its proportions and identify an assumption that could change the recommendation.

05 / Keep the evidence

What your record should contain

  • Versioned workload specification and seed
  • Query mix and data-distribution assumptions
  • Results for each workload and operation type

06 / Interpret

What the evidence can support

A synthetic workload represents the declared scenario. Its representativeness needs evidence; a single composite score can hide tradeoffs between operations.
