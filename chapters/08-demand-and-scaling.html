What happens as demand grows?

We’ll increase the amount of data and the demand on our example systems. A result from a quiet test may change as customers grow or become busier. You’ll learn to find the demand each option can sustain while still meeting the customer’s targets.

This is a chapter outline. The worked example and executable lab are planned. You can use the design task below to start your benchmark brief.

What you will learn

  • Plan tests that increase data size, request arrival rate or requests in progress.
  • Identify the capacity that meets the service target.
  • Investigate a suspected bottleneck through a controlled change.

01 / The question

Start with a customer decision

Earlier chapters examined results for a stated workload. Now we’ll ask how those results change as the work grows. Capacity here means the amount of work a deployment can sustain while meeting its response-time and correctness requirements.

Imagine a customer preparing for more orders and a seasonal rush. The engine that answers fastest in a small, quiet test may reach its limits sooner as demand increases. We need to find where waits, errors or unfinished work begin to grow.

We’ll increase data size and request demand in separate tests so we can interpret each change. Later tests against server deployments will also vary concurrency, the number of requests in progress at once. The goal is to identify useful operating limits and investigate what constrains them.

02 / Worked example · planned

The comparison to build

Extend the orders benchmark across increasing data sizes, then use native/server runs to examine concurrent demand. Keep these sweeps separate so the cause of a change remains interpretable.

03 / Experiment · planned

How the lab will work

  1. State latency, correctness and error targets before increasing load.
  2. Plot achieved work, response time and backlog as demand rises.
  3. Change a suspected limiting resource and repeat the relevant points.

The executable lab, reference outputs and progressive hints will be added when this chapter is implemented.

04 / Design task

Start your benchmark brief

Recommend a tested operating range and identify which growth assumption requires another experiment.

05 / Keep the evidence

What your record should contain

  • Sweep values, fixed conditions and service targets
  • Resource observations, backlog and completed work
  • Repeated measurements around a limit or crossover

06 / Interpret

What the evidence can support

An untested maximum is not established capacity. Explain whether backlog remains bounded and whether results include the effects of background work over a suitable duration.
