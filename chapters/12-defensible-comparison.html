Present a comparison you can defend

We’ll bring the workload, measurements and cost assumptions together in a customer recommendation. You’ll learn to explain what was tested, why it matters and what could change the decision, while giving another person enough evidence to check your conclusion.

This is a chapter outline. The worked example and executable lab are planned. You can use the design task below to start your benchmark brief.

What you will learn

  • Produce a benchmark brief, self-checking workload and repeatable runner.
  • Connect a conditional technical finding to a customer decision.
  • Review somebody else’s evidence and propose a fair follow-up test.

01 / The question

Start with a customer decision

A customer needs to choose an option, and another technical reviewer needs to understand the evidence behind your advice. This final chapter brings together the comparison skills developed throughout the book.

We’ll assemble a benchmark brief, a short statement of the decision, workload, requirements and planned measurements. Alongside it, we’ll keep the setup instructions, correctness checks, raw observations and analysis needed to repeat the experiment.

You’ll use this material to write a concise recommendation for a database, an event system or a complete pipeline. A review of another comparison will help you practise finding missing evidence and proposing a useful next test. The aim is a claim whose conditions, tradeoffs and practical value are clear.

02 / Worked example · planned

The comparison to build

Assemble the evidence developed throughout the book into a short comparison brief with a reproducibility package. Choose a database, broker or complete pipeline.

03 / Experiment · planned

How the lab will work

  1. Run a clean repetition from documented setup and verify the expected outputs.
  2. Check every claim against the saved conditions, observations and cost assumptions.
  3. Have a reviewer challenge workload choice, guarantees, uncertainty and the relevance of the recommendation.

The executable lab, reference outputs and progressive hints will be added when this chapter is implemented.

04 / Design task

Start your benchmark brief

Submit your own benchmark and report, then review a separate published comparison. Propose an experiment to address its most consequential missing evidence.

05 / Keep the evidence

What your record should contain

  • Decision question, acceptance targets and workload specification
  • Runnable setup, checks, raw observations and versioned configuration
  • Customer-facing claim, conditions, uncertainty and cost model

06 / Interpret

What the evidence can support

Keep the recommendation within the evidence. Explain tradeoffs and unresolved questions, and distinguish measured findings from estimates and assumptions.
