Beyond the average

We’ll look beyond a typical request time to examine long waits, failed requests and completed work. Customers experience all three. You’ll learn to compare systems against response-time and completion targets that describe the service they need.

This is a chapter outline. The worked example and executable lab are planned. You can use the design task below to start your benchmark brief.

What you will learn

  • Define where request timing starts and stops, and which requests a summary includes.
  • Compare the work completed with the time requests take.
  • Account for failures, timeouts and unfinished requests.

01 / The question

Start with a customer decision

A typical response time helps describe an experience, but it leaves out the slowest requests and the work that never finishes. Two systems can look similar by that one number while providing different service during a busy period.

We’ll use latency for the elapsed time between defined request events, such as sending a request and receiving its full answer. Throughput is the amount of work completed per unit of time. Reading them together helps us ask how much work a system completes while keeping waits acceptable.

Imagine two order screens with similar typical response times. One occasionally makes users wait much longer and fails more requests during a burst. We’ll inspect individual request records, including errors and unfinished work, to compare the experience each system delivers.

03 / Experiment · planned

How the lab will work

  1. Collect individual request timestamps under a declared arrival model.
  2. Report typical and tail latency with offered and achieved throughput.
  3. Inspect what changes when timeouts and outstanding requests are accounted for.

04 / Design task

Start your benchmark brief

Choose metrics for an interactive lookup and a batch report. Explain why the same headline metric may not serve both decisions.

05 / Keep the evidence

What your record should contain

  • Request-level observations and measurement points
  • Error, timeout and incomplete-work counts
  • Percentile method and throughput observation window

06 / Interpret

What the evidence can support

A percentile describes a specified population. Omitting failed or unfinished requests can change the story. Do not average percentiles or substitute batch averages for request observations.
