From benchmark results to capacity and cost

We’ll use benchmark evidence to estimate the deployment each option needs, then compare its cost. A lower price per server may still lead to a higher overall bill. You’ll learn to connect measured performance to a cost comparison with visible assumptions.

This is a chapter outline. The worked example and executable lab are planned. You can use the design task below to start your benchmark brief.

What you will learn

  • Translate measured sustainable performance into a capacity model.
  • Distinguish measured facts, claims and assumptions.
  • Compare total cost and explore sensitivity to uncertain inputs.

01 / The question

Start with a customer decision

Knowing which option is faster helps, but a buying decision also depends on the resources needed to deliver the required service. We’ll connect the capacity measured in earlier chapters to a deployment design and a cost estimate.

Suppose one option has a lower price per server but needs more servers to handle the customer’s demand. Storage, networking, licences and operational effort may change the comparison again. Total cost of ownership (TCO) brings the relevant costs together over a stated period.

We’ll compare deployments that meet the same workload, response-time and availability requirements. Each performance input will point to benchmark evidence; prices and other assumptions will have their own sources. You’ll then explore which uncertain inputs could change the recommendation.

02 / Worked example · planned

The comparison to build

Take native/server benchmark records into a capacity and cost model. Link each performance input to its evidence, then add separately sourced prices and operating assumptions.

Companion

Read Sizing and TCO for capacity planning, cost models and uncertainty analysis.

03 / Experiment · planned

How the lab will work

  1. Size for demand, headroom and availability while checking where replication is already included.
  2. Account for compute, storage, requests, networking, licences and operational effort.
  3. Vary demand, prices and performance assumptions and inspect whether the recommendation changes.

The executable lab, reference outputs and progressive hints will be added when this chapter is implemented.

04 / Design task

Start your benchmark brief

Compare two complete deployments over a stated period. Identify the uncertain input most likely to change the buying decision.

05 / Keep the evidence

What your record should contain

  • Performance evidence and capacity/deployment assumptions
  • Dated regional prices, discounts and operating assumptions
  • Cost model, sensitivity results and costs of failures or missed targets

06 / Interpret

What the evidence can support

Use the Sizing and TCO companion for deeper modelling. Browser timings alone do not establish infrastructure cost. Pipeline models must include workers, connectors, retention, retries and recovery capacity.
